How To Use This App

The big picture: Find a Grave has lists of headstones people want photographed at cemeteries across the country. Many local municipalities (like the City of St. Louis, MI for Oak Grove Cemetery) publish their actual burial records online via BS&A Online, including section/lot location. This app cross-references the two so you can walk any cemetery efficiently.

It's not tied to any one cemetery — paste in data from whichever Find a Grave photo request page and whichever municipal cemetery records you're working with.

Why doesn't it scrape live?

Both Find a Grave and BS&A Online block automated requests from web browsers (Find a Grave returns a 403 error; BS&A requires interactive form submission). A browser-based scraper would also be blocked by CORS security. The realistic and reliable approach is a one-time paste — takes about 10 minutes, then the app does all the matching.

Getting data from Find a Grave

Easiest method — download the spreadsheet:

  1. Go to findagrave.com and navigate to the cemetery you're working with.
  2. Click "View Photo Requests" (or use the URL pattern /photo-request/search/cemetery/[ID]).
  3. Look for a Download button on the photo requests page. Find a Grave will give you an .xlsx file (up to 1,000 records at a time).
  4. Drop that file directly onto the "Drop a file here" zone in the Find a Grave paste box. You don't need Excel — the app parses the file in your browser.

Alternative — copy and paste:

  1. For each page of results, select the list of entries (each one has a name, dates, and a memorial ID).
  2. Paste the raw text into the Find a Grave box. Junk lines get filtered out.
  3. The memorial ID is the long number in each entry's link (e.g., 16714155 in /memorial/16714155/). The app uses it to deep-link back so you can fulfill the request right at the headstone.

Example of a line the parser understands:

John A. Smith  1882-1945  Memorial #16714155

Getting data from BS&A Online

Best method — ask the City Clerk: the staff side of BS&A has a built-in "export to spreadsheet" button. Most clerks will email you the entire cemetery roster as an .xlsx or .csv in a few minutes. Drop that file directly onto the BS&A box and you're done.

Manual method — copy and paste:

  1. Go to bsaonline.com and find the municipality that manages the cemetery you're researching.
  2. Open that municipality's Cemetery Management Search.
  3. To pull a lot of records at once, leave the name field blank or search broadly (e.g. surname "A%").
  4. Copy the results table (right-click → Select all, or drag-select the rows).
  5. Paste into the BS&A box. The parser pulls names, dates, and section/block/lot info.

The downside of the manual method is that BS&A's public search hides plot location until you click each record individually. So the spreadsheet export from the clerk is by far the better option if you can get it.

Example of a line the parser understands:

Smith, John A.   b. 1882   d. 1945   Section B  Lot 14  Grave 3

Note: not every cemetery is in BS&A. If yours isn't, any text-based grave record list (from a township website, a transcription project, etc.) works — the parser just needs names, years, and location fields.

Matching logic

  • EXACT — surname, first name, and at least one matching year (birth or death).
  • STRONG — surname + first initial + a matching year.
  • PARTIAL — surname matches and dates are close, but ambiguous (manual confirmation suggested).
  • Records that don't match are still shown in the All Records tab so you can spot misses.

At the cemetery

  • Open the Walking List tab. It groups graves by section, so you can clear one section at a time without backtracking.
  • Each match shows name, dates, and section/lot.
  • Tap the dark "Open Find a Grave" button on a match — it opens that exact photo request in a new tab so you can upload your photo on the spot.
  • Check off completed photos. Progress saves automatically in your browser.
  • The app works offline once loaded — save the page to your phone, or just keep this tab open before you lose signal.

Saving the app for offline use

This is a single HTML file. To use it offline:

  1. On a desktop: just download the file and double-click it. It opens in any browser, no install needed.
  2. On a phone: open the file in your browser at home, paste your data, then leave the tab open. Your data and progress save to local storage and survive a closed tab as long as you don't clear your browser data.
  3. You can also use Export Progress to save a backup .json file, and Import Progress to restore it.
