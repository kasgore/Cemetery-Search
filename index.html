How this app finds graves

Every photo request is located by the best available evidence, in this order:

  1. GPS the memorial's own pin (rare — most requests have none);
  2. LOT the exact lot position from the city plat maps, georeferenced with 450+ GPS-tagged memorials (usually within a few graves);
  3. BLOCK the block/row when the lot isn't drawn (10–30 m);
  4. SECTION the section area only (~40 m) — use neighbors to close in.

The colored dot on each card shows which level you have, with an accuracy estimate in meters.

Field technique

  • Use the neighbors list. Tap Neighbors on any target: people buried in the same lot and the adjacent lots, with a 📷 marking ones whose stones are already photographed on Find a Grave. Walk to a photographed neighbor (tap it to see the stone's photo), then count stones — the spouse is very often in the same lot even if unmarked.
  • Lot geometry: lots climb south to north at about 3 m per lot number; odd lots sit west, even lots east, in pairs. Blocks are strips labeled at one end. Graves are ~2.5 ft wide, lots hold 4–8.
  • No stone? That's a real answer: photograph the spot in context with surrounding stones and note "unmarked" in the caption when fulfilling — or mark No stone here and flag the request on Find a Grave so the requester learns the outcome.
  • Reading worn stones: raking light (sun ~30° across the face) is everything. East-facing stones read best late morning; west-facing early afternoon. A spray bottle of water raises contrast; a mirror or foil board throws light on shaded faces.
  • Kit: water spray, soft brush, grass clippers, trowel, mirror/foil, battery pack.

Compass & GPS notes

  • Phone GPS is good to 3–8 m outdoors — it gets you to the right lot/row, then your eyes take over.
  • The arrow needs the compass permission (it will ask on first use). Hold the phone flat; keep away from cars and metal fences. True-north correction (−6.6° declination) is applied automatically.
  • GPS only works over HTTPS — use the installed app or the github.io address, not a copied file.
