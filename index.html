How this app finds graves

When a request has any plot or GPS evidence, it's located by the best of it — the colored chip on each card names the level and an honest accuracy radius:

  1. GPS PIN the grave's own pin, or its memorial's (±25 ft);
  2. LOT the exact lot — at Oak Grove from the city plat maps (georeferenced with 580+ GPS-tagged stones); everywhere else from GPS-tagged memorials in the same lot. Multiple agreeing pins beat one lone pin, so the chip shows the pin count;
  3. NEAR LOT / BLOCK pins in adjacent lots or the block/row area (±45–150 ft);
  4. SECTION the section area only — the radius shown is that section's real measured extent.

Most requests (about 3 in 4) have no plot on record anywhere. For those the app gives you what an experienced hunter uses instead: the burial register when one exists (12 cemeteries have one — matched by name and dates; a "verify" badge means check it's the same person), and family leads under the Neighbors button — same-surname burials with known spots, because spouses usually share the lot.

Field technique

  • Use the neighbors list. Tap Neighbors on any target: people buried in the same lot and the adjacent lots, with a 📷 marking ones whose stones are already photographed on Find a Grave. Walk to a photographed neighbor (tap it to see the stone's photo), then count stones — the spouse is very often in the same lot even if unmarked.
  • Lot geometry at Oak Grove: lots climb south to north at about 3 m per lot number; odd lots sit west, even lots east, in pairs; blocks are strips labeled at one end. Other cemeteries number their own way — trust the dots and the neighbors, not this rule. Graves are ~2.5 ft wide; lots hold 4–8.
  • No stone? That's a real answer: photograph the spot in context with surrounding stones and note "unmarked" in the caption when fulfilling — or mark No stone here and flag the request on Find a Grave so the requester learns the outcome.
  • Reading worn stones: raking light (sun ~30° across the face) is everything. East-facing stones read best late morning; west-facing early afternoon. A spray bottle of water raises contrast; a mirror or foil board throws light on shaded faces.
  • Kit: water spray, soft brush, grass clippers, trowel, mirror/foil, battery pack.

Compass & GPS notes

  • Phone GPS is good to 3–8 m outdoors — it gets you to the right lot/row, then your eyes take over.
  • The arrow needs the compass permission (it will ask on first use). Hold the phone flat; keep away from cars and metal fences. The true-north correction for this area is applied automatically.
  • GPS only works over HTTPS: use the installed app, or the home server's https:// address (accept its certificate warning once per device). Plain http:// shows everything but can't navigate.

Badges & marks

  • register — plot from a city burial register (more precise than Find a Grave's).
  • register match — verify — the register row was matched by name/date similarity; make sure it's the same person.
  • verify plot — Find a Grave and the register disagree about where this grave is; check both.
  • vet — veteran. 📷 — that stone already has a photo (a visual anchor). 📝 — you left a field note. 📍 — you saved GPS at the grave.
